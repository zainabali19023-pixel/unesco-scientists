Integrated Analysis

Countries were ranked separately within each of three source-level indicators: ACLED conflict-violence events, EM-DAT natural-disaster events, and WHO COVID-19 deaths per 100,000 population. The top 15 in each ranking were then compared, after normalizing country names across sources.

Three countries rank among the top 15 in all three indicators: Brazil, Colombia and Mexico. This reflects breadth of exposure, not peak severity in any single category: none of the three tops any individual list (Afghanistan has more conflict-violence events than Mexico, the United States has far more disasters than Brazil, and Hungary has a higher COVID-19 death rate than any of the three). Rather, these are the only three countries that stay inside the top-15 cutoff on all three measures at once, as shown below.

Data Sources

ACLED - Conflict-Violence Against Women Professionals

The Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project provides event-level records of political violence and demonstrations, each accompanied by a short English-language narrative description, location, date and source. ACLED is the only one of the three sources to include per-event text, and is accordingly the sole basis for the "women professionals" framing applied throughout this dashboard's conflict-related analyses.

EM-DAT - Climate & Health Disasters

EM-DAT, the international disaster database maintained by CRED (UCLouvain), supplies 3,137 natural-disaster records spanning 2019–2026 across 193 countries, covering floods, storms, earthquakes, extreme temperature events, wildfires, droughts, epidemics (cholera and other outbreaks), volcanic activity and related hazard types. Records are aggregated at the country/location level and report deaths, injuries, and numbers affected or rendered homeless. EM-DAT contains no per-event narrative text and makes no reference to individuals or professions.

WHO COVID-19 Global Table

The World Health Organization's cumulative COVID-19 case and death table reports counts by country, including rates per 100,000 population, for 241 countries as a single snapshot as of the report date rather than as a time series. As with EM-DAT, the table contains no per-event data and no reference to individuals or professions.

Final Keyword Vocabulary

Each ACLED event was required to match at least one of fourteen professional-role keywords:

university, health worker, nurse, professor, faculty, university staff, academic, researcher, doctor, medical staff, scholar, laboratory, research center, scientist

An additional eight topical phrases were specified as intended framing keywords: women scientist, women researcher, female scientist, female researcher, women in science, women in STEM, gender in science, gender in research.

Data Processing & Quality Assurance

ACLED: Five-Stage Refinement

Stage 1: Broad Keyword Matching
An initial pass identified 24,830 events containing any mention of a scientist-related keyword in combination with a women-related keyword.

Stage 2: Refined Filtering (5,708 events)
False-positive patterns, place names and broad gender terms not indicative of a professional context were removed, and a requirement for professional-role focus and crisis context was introduced.

Stage 3: Ultra-Refined Filtering (5,229 events)
Further removal of place-name false positives, passive-victimhood patterns and violence occurring outside a professional context.

Stage 4: Final Role Vocabulary (3,368 events)
The finalized fourteen-keyword professional-role vocabulary (see "Final Keyword Vocabulary," above) was reapplied, dropping teacher, instructor, surgeon, healthcare worker, physician and hospital staff from the earlier working list and reinstating nurse. Events retaining no qualifying role were excluded.

Stage 5: Conflict & Violence Filter (236 events)
Of the Stage-4 dataset, 86.9% (2,927 events) remained classified as "Protests" (demonstrations rather than violence). This final stage retains only ACLED's political-violence categories (violence against civilians, explosions/remote violence, battles) and excludes protests, riots and strategic developments, removing 3,132 events (93.0%).

EM-DAT Processing

  • Country names were normalized to match ACLED's naming conventions (eight countries remapped; for example, "Democratic Republic of the Congo" → "Democratic Republic of Congo" and "Iran (Islamic Republic of)" → "Iran")
  • Only 229 of 3,137 records (7%) carry precise event coordinates; the remaining 91% are plotted at country centroids
  • Dates were constructed from separate Year/Month/Day fields; missing month or day values defaulted to 01 (31 records missing month, 313 missing day)

WHO COVID-19 Processing

  • Country names were normalized to match ACLED's naming conventions (eight countries remapped, including "occupied Palestinian territory, including east Jerusalem" → "Palestine")
  • Blank case/death cells were treated as not reported (null) rather than as zero

Cross-Referencing

All three sources were joined on normalized country name to support the Integrated Analysis presented above. This constitutes a geographic and temporal overlay rather than a merged event dataset: ACLED, EM-DAT and WHO records are never combined into a single row implying a direct causal or individual-level link.

Limitations

ACLED language and coverage bias: ACLED's records reflect English-language media coverage; some regions and languages are consequently underrepresented, and keyword specificity cannot capture every relevant context.

The conflict-violence filter narrows scope but does not invalidate the remainder of the dataset: the 236-event conflict-violence set deliberately excludes protests, riots and strategic developments (93.0% of the Stage-4 dataset); these events still occurred, but do not constitute political violence.

EM-DAT and WHO carry no individual-level link to women professionals: EM-DAT's full text fields were checked for role and gender keywords (school, hospital, teacher, nurse, university, women, girl, and others), returning zero matches across all 3,137 records. Both sources are included solely as geographic and temporal context, not as additional women-professional incidents.

WHO COVID-19 data represent a single snapshot rather than a time series: cumulative totals are reported as of the report date only, and therefore cannot show infection or mortality trends over the 2019–2025 study window in the way the conflict-violence data can.

EM-DAT excludes COVID-19 as a special case: EM-DAT's own methodology omits COVID-19 owing to its unprecedented scale; its "Epidemic" category (117 records) therefore covers other outbreaks, such as cholera, only, and COVID-19 coverage is supplied by the WHO table.

Country-name joins are best-effort: each source pair required eight to ten manual remaps, and disputed or differently scoped territories (for example, Palestine, Taiwan, and the Hong Kong and Macao Special Administrative Regions) carry additional uncertainty.

The "top 15" and "compounding/triple-crisis" thresholds used in the Integrated Analysis above are chosen for interpretability rather than derived through a statistical procedure; a country's absence from either grouping does not indicate low risk, only that it fell outside the top 15 by raw event count (or, for WHO data, deaths per 100,000 population) in one or more of the three source rankings. These thresholds should be read as a heuristic for surfacing candidates for closer qualitative reading, not as a formal, statistically validated risk score.
